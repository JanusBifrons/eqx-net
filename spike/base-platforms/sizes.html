Cables go up the leaf's seams

A plate is a leaf of 7 hexes with the connector in the middle hex. Cables leave the connector at a corner of the middle hex, run along the seams between hexes, and come in the same way at the next plate's connector. They never cross a hex, so nothing built in a hex sits on a cable, and the middle hex stays a clear yard round the connector.

4 plates, linked in a ring, drawn the same both ways. The dashed cyan hex is each connector's yard. On the right the cables follow the dark seams: the seams between plates and the seams between the petals of a leaf.

19 or 49: two ways to grow

Both start the same: 1 hex, then 7 hexes (a hex with a ring round it). They differ in the step after that.

A keeps adding rings: 7 plus a second ring of 12 makes 19, which is still one big hexagon with one connector in the middle. B repeats the flower: 7 plates of 7 make 49, which is a flower of flowers with a connector in each plate (7 in all). Same scale throughout. Cyan diamonds are connectors.

How each one sits on a normal base

A normal base is tiled in 7-hex plates (the grey flowers, each with a connector in the middle). Here's what happens when you put each one down.

A, 19 hexes, doesn't line up with the plates. It takes 2 hexes from each of the 6 plates around it. The red hexes are what is left of those plates: 5 hexes each, in odd shapes. Their connectors still work. B, 49 hexes, covers exactly 7 whole plates, so the plates around it are untouched. It's much bigger, and the 7 connectors under it are built into it. On a huge base, where plates are already 19 hexes, A is exactly one plate.
