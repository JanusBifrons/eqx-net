Base Platforms

Bases stop being loose buildings over the starfield. You lay a deck of tiles, the deck carries power, ships fly down its streets, and buildings sit on top. These are wireframes: shapes, layout and rules, not final art.

Buildings keep their shape, inside a hex round 8b · new

Buildings keep today's shapes. Each one sits inside a slot (a third of a hex, a whole hex, or a 7-hex plate), and nothing hangs over the slot's edge. The hex is sized to fit today's buildings, so nothing is redrawn except the biggest few. Those take a whole plate and get redesigned to be built round the plate's connector. Roads are tiles you lay yourself.

Everything is drawn at today's footprint, to scale. A hex 820 u flat to flat is the smallest that fits the reactor, battery bank, capital and super battery, so all of those stay 1 hex. In a plate building, the ringed diamond in the middle is the plate's connector, part of the building: cables plug into it, and it is still the plate's anchor.

Every structure in the game today, in its slot

Measured from the structure catalogue. 14 structures are small, 19 fit one hex, and 8 are too big for a hex, so they go plate-sized. Those 8 are drawn as today's footprint over the plate for now. Their redesign builds the connector into the middle. The siege gun is long and thin (300 × 900 u): it is too long for any hex, so it takes a plate too.

The next size after a 7-hex plate: you choose

Two ways to fit something bigger than a plate (today only the large teleporter, 2 120 u across, which just misses a 7-hex plate: a plate fits about 1 890 u; later, bigger buildings) on a normal base. A, 19 hexes: one connector, and the building is no bigger than it needs to be, but it takes 2 hexes from each of the 6 plates round it. Those plates are left 5 hexes each, with odd shapes. B, 49 hexes: exactly 7 whole plates, so nothing round it is cut. It covers 7 connectors, all built into it, so it can be repaired plate by plate. It is much bigger: the large teleporter would leave a lot of room inside. On a huge base the plates are already 19 hexes, so A is exactly one plate there.

A normal base, 7-hex plates. The roads wind where the player laid them, and gates (green) appear where a road reaches the fence. The capital is now a 1-hex building. An assembly plant, a shipyard and a teleporter each take a whole plate with the connector built in. Elsewhere, buildings sit in their own hexes round the plate's connector. Most of the deck is still empty.

A huge base with 19-hex plates, drawn smaller. Fewer, bigger plates mean fewer connectors to look after. A 7-hex building can still sit on a 19-hex plate: it centres over the connector and leaves a ring of 12 hexes round it for 1-hex buildings. Here the deep core rig and the shipyard do that, and the large teleporter takes a whole 19-hex plate.

What changes from round 8

  • No redraw: every building keeps today's art and shape. Only the 8 biggest are redesigned, round their connector.
  • The capital, reactor and battery bank stay 1 hex, so they don't become plate-sized.
  • Plate buildings and connectors are one object: tap the shipyard to see its plate's health.
  • Small buildings leave space round them in their hex, since rectangles don't fill a hex. That gap is the price of keeping today's shapes.
  • The jump from 1 hex to 7 is still big: the ore processing complex (840 u) only just misses one hex.

Sizes in numbers

  • 1 hex: 820 u flat to flat (today's tiles are 640 u).
  • Small slot (a third of a hex): fits up to about 300 × 300 u.
  • 7-hex plate: about 2 460 u across.
  • 19 hexes: about 4 100 u across. 49 hexes: about 5 700 u across.
  • Too big for one hex today: shipyard 960 × 600, freight dock 1000 × 400, assembly plant 900 × 640, ore processing 840 × 600, deep core rig 760, teleporter and transit pad 800, siege gun 300 × 900.

Hexagons, cheap deck, room to grow round 7 · new

Space is cheap, so deck and fence are cheap. What makes a big base expensive is defending a long fence, not building it. Most bases are mostly empty deck, ready to grow into; only bases squeezed into an asteroid field end up packed. And the grid is hexagons.

A young base in open space. The fence was laid wide and cheap, so most of the deck inside is empty and waiting. Buildings cluster round the capital and thin out towards the rim. Each plate is a flower of 7 hexes with its connector in the middle, and every hex in a plate touches its connector, so building cables are one short hop. Cables join each plate to all of its neighbours. Cyan pulses are power from the capital. Red-cored hexes are towers in the fence line, and green marks gates where the three roads leave.

The same rules in an asteroid field

The clearing is small, so the deck is too, and the base fills every hex. Dense packing comes from where you are, not from the rules.

Why hexagons

  • All six neighbours are the same distance away, so cables, fences and roads need no diagonal special case. The corner cuts on the square fence go away.
  • Fences and deck edges come out rounded rather than boxy.
  • A 7-hex plate has one middle hex touching all the others, which suits the connector you picked.
  • Round things fit better: teleporters, the alien gate, reactors.
  • Rectangular buildings (shipyard, freight dock, solar) leave more spare deck at their corners than they would on squares.
  • Roads run in three directions, 60° apart, so the street pattern is a triangle net rather than city blocks.

The hex size ladder

Earlier rounds below are drawn on squares. Their rules carry over to hexes: fence and tower tiles, gates where a road crosses a fence, and one connector in the middle of each plate, now a 7-hex plate.

Connectors and cables you can follow round 6 · new

The deck is the floor, not the wiring. Power and minerals still travel on connectors and cables, today's cable look and flow colours, laid out on the deck's grid so the web is tidy and you can trace where everything goes.

Same base as the fence picture below. Diamonds: connectors. Thick lines: cables between connectors, drawn overhead with their cradles. Thin lines: each building's own short cable to its nearest connector. Cyan pulses: power spreading out from the capital. Orange pulses: ore from the mining annex heading to processing, crossing the fence line. Switch between the two layouts with the buttons.

Middle of each plate

Your pick
  • The plate's connector is its anchor: one thing to select, repair and rebuild, so nothing new is added.
  • A neat square web, one cable per plate edge; easy to follow by eye.
  • The middle tile of each plate stays clear as a small yard for the connector, so one building slot per plate is used up. Where a street runs through the middle, the connector sits on the road's edge and the cable runs along the road.

Corners of each plate

Not picked
  • Cables run along the seams between plates, framing each block like streetlight lines, and connectors sit at the crossings.
  • No building slots lost: connectors sit in the gaps where four tiles meet.
  • Each connector is shared by up to four plates, so the plate's anchor becomes a separate thing again: two objects per plate to look after.
  • Buildings in the middle of a block need longer cables out to a corner, so more lines fan out across the block.

Laid with the deck

When you lay a plate its connector comes with it and cables to the neighbouring connectors, so you never wire a base by hand. You can still add your own connectors and cables on top, as today.

Buildings plug in

Each building gets a short cable from its socket to the nearest connector, so you can see what it draws from.

Cut a cable, lose the flow

Destroy a connector or cable and the pulses stop at the break. Anything cut off from a reactor goes dark, as it does now.

Snap grid, fence tiles, road gates round 5b

The deck is a grid, so buildings come in sizes that fit it. Fences are a kind of deck tile you lay where you want the wall: plain, or with a tower built in. Wherever a road crosses a fence line it becomes a gate, and ships arrive by teleporter instead of a landing pad.

The size ladder

One tile is 640 u, the same as today's row spacing. Every building has a slot size, and it snaps into whole tiles of that size. The footprint only has to fit inside the slot, so the spare deck around it is the breathing room. Small things share a tile, four to a tile.

Fence is a tile

A fence tile is a deck tile with a shield emitter in it. Lay a line of them and the wall joins up between them. Nothing to build on it.

You choose where

Fence the whole rim, just the core, or leave an annex outside. Nothing is fenced unless you put it there.

Tower tiles

A fence tile with a turret tower built into it. The barrel sits on the outer face of the wall, so it fires out without crossing its own shield.

Segments can be hit

Each tile's length of wall takes fire and can go down, leaving a gap until it's repaired from that plate's anchor.

Gates where roads cross

Lay a fence line across a street and that street tile becomes a gate. Friendly ships fly through; hostiles don't.

Teleporters, not pads

Where the old drawings had landing pads, there are teleporters (a 2 × 2 slot). Ships arrive on the deck through them, or fly in through a gate.

Turrets: where do the guns go?

Today a fence blocks its owner's own fire as well as the enemy's (your ruling, 2 Nov; locked by a test). So a turret inside the fence can't shoot out, which is why NPC bases put their guns on bastions outside it. You leaned to towers, and said they make more sense as a fence tile type; that's what A now shows. B and C stay for comparison.

A · Tower tiles in the fence line

Your lean
  • A tower tile is a fence tile with a turret built into it, like a tower in a castle wall. The barrel sits on the outer face, so the fence rule stays as it is.
  • You place them where you want guns, as part of laying the fence.
  • Guns sit on whatever line you chose to defend.
  • They can only shoot outwards. A ship that gets inside through a gap is safe from them.
  • They're exposed: the enemy can shoot them without breaking the fence.

B · Bastions outside the fence

Today's NPC pattern
  • A bastion tile is deck that the fence goes behind, not around. Turrets on it fire freely.
  • Same as NPC bases already do, so the grower barely changes.
  • More to place, and bastions stick out of the outline.
  • Turrets fully unshielded.

C · One-way fence

Changes your 2 Nov rule
  • Turrets go anywhere on the deck and shoot out through the fence. Enemy shots still stop at it.
  • Simplest to place: a turret is just another small building.
  • Turrets behind a fence with no way to hit them make bases very hard to crack.
  • Reverses the rule that a fence blocks its owner's fire.

Earlier rounds follow. Where they show landing pads, free placement or light snapping, round 5 above replaces them.

What's wrong today recap

  • The starfield and nebulae show straight through a base, so it never feels like a place you arrive at.
  • Bases are small and cramped. There's no structure to the layout: no main route in, no back streets.
  • Connectors are a separate chore and draw a web of lines that says nothing about the shape of the base.

Today's NPC bases put rows of buildings 640 u apart, which is barely more than a Reactor is wide (600 u). Cables can be as short as 67 u, so neighbours almost touch.

Three ways to give a base a floor concepts

These are the three ideas from the brief, in the order you talked through them. Same buildings in each, so only the floor rule changes.

A · A pad under every building

Drop
  • Gaps everywhere. It reads as many small rafts, not one place.
  • Connectors still needed between pads.
  • Nothing new to place.

B · Connectors grow the slab

Keep one part
  • Round blobs from connector ranges. Hard to make look built rather than grown.
  • No streets unless the player fakes them with gaps.
  • The good part: the core lays a big starter slab, so a new base already has ground.

C · Lay deck tiles, build on top

Recommended
  • Square grid, no overlaps, edges join up. Always looks tidy.
  • Tiles carry the power, so connectors go away as a separate thing.
  • Street tiles make the main avenue and back alleys you described.
  • A new thing to place, and a new rule: only build on deck.

When the deck is hit destruction

Tiles are part of the base, so they take damage like anything else. The deck never shrinks by itself; it only loses tiles that are destroyed.

Anchors: what you click to repair and rebuild new

The deck is floor, so you can't easily tap it, target it or see its health. Every piece of deck therefore hangs off an anchor: a small structure in the same layer as connectors and buildings. You select it, repair it, see its health bar, and rebuild from it. The question is how much deck one anchor holds.

One anchor per tile

Too many
  • Damage is exact: one hit, one tile.
  • A citadel needs about 420 anchors, more than the 400 NPC structures a sector allows today.
  • Floor is covered in sockets.

Pylons at tile corners

Closest to your first idea
  • Connectors lay the floor between them, as in your first idea.
  • Even more entities (about 465 for a citadel).
  • Lose one pylon and four tiles are in doubt. Hard to read.

One anchor per plate

Recommended
  • A plate is 3 × 3 tiles (about 1.9 km). A citadel is about 50 plates, so 50 anchors.
  • The anchor is the plate's health bar, its power node and its rebuild point.
  • Damage is coarser: a plate dies as a whole.

Rebuilding a plate

Light snapping

Near a tile edge or centre line (within about 40 u), the ghost pulls onto it and an amber guide shows what it lined up with.

Anywhere else it stays where you put it. Snapping never stops a placement; it only helps.

Rules for the prototype proposal

Ground first

Everything except deck must sit fully on deck. Buildings are placed freely with light snapping to tile edges and centres, and can span tiles. They can't hang off the edge or sit on a street.

Plates have anchors

Deck is laid in plates, each held by one anchor you can select, repair and rebuild. Destroy the anchor and the plate breaks; rebuild the anchor and the plate comes back as a blueprint.

Core lays a starter slab

Placing the first core drops a ready-made slab with a short avenue and a pad, so a new base has somewhere to build straight away.

Cables stay visible

Power flows from the core along connectors and cables laid with the deck (round 6), not invisibly through the tiles. Anything with no cable path to a core is unpowered and shows it.

Grid, no overlap

Tiles snap to one grid per base and edges join. Tiles can only be laid touching existing deck, so a base is always one piece when built.

Streets are for flying

Street tiles can't be built on. They're the lanes AI pilots and the navigation map prefer through a base.

Ships fly over the deck

The deck is floor, not a wall. Ships cross it freely and only hit buildings, as now.
