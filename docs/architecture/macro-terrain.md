# Macro terrain: asteroid fields, wreck fields, nebulae (design, 2026-11-15)

<!-- showcase-exempt: Stage 0 harness only; the terrain-stress shot lands in slice 0d -->

Status: **plan v2 (the cut field) approved 2026-11-15; slices 0a, 1a, A1 and A2 done.** The plan file is `tender-humming-whisper.md` in the plans folder.

## Why

The game has no macro terrain. A sector holds a few small rock layouts, nebulae are scenery only, and AI pilots fly straight lines that ignore rocks, walls and wrecks. The owner wants:

- **Asteroid fields** spanning hundreds of km, with giant blockers and hundreds of thousands of small rocks. Each field has capital lanes, an open core, pockets, and rich mining outposts.
- **Old-battle wreck fields.**
- **Nebulae** spanning several sectors, which slow target locks.
- **One route-finder** for AI pilots and the player autopilot.

Look and feel, and performance, are the guiding stars. Measurements set the limits, never numbers invented in advance.

## The cut field (plan v2)

After look tests with the owner (2026-11-15), a field is no longer thousands of placed rocks: evenly scattered rocks "read like a pattern". A field is **one giant convex shape cut up like a cake** (a fracture), in `src/core/terrain/fieldLayout.ts` (`slicedField`):

1. **Lanes first.** One or two straight strips run end to end. Their walls are the first cuts, and the strip is removed, so lanes have clean walls.
2. **Recursive cuts.** Each piece is cut across its longest dimension, at a seeded point 35–65 % along it, with a little angle jitter. That avoids slivers.
3. **Where cutting stops.** A piece stops when it is rock-sized (4–20 fighter lengths, seeded), or rarely at 1–2 battleship lengths (the rare big rocks).
4. **Streets.** Each piece shrinks toward its centre, leaving streets about two fighter widths wide at the core. Streets widen toward the rim, where pieces also drop out, giving a slow thinning from core to edge.
5. **The core.** The middle is a hole.
6. **Shape.** Edges are roughened by midpoint displacement, then one smooth two-scale warp bends every outline. Neighbours bend together, so streets curve.

**Look and size.** The field is about 300 km across, which is never fully on screen at any zoom. The full field is 113,881 rocks; cutting it all in one go takes about 8 s.

**Determinism and laziness.**
- Every cut is seeded by the piece's path in the cut tree. A child's path mixes the parent's path with the depth and the side (`childPath`), so paths never collide.
- Any region can be cut on its own: pieces outside a view box stop early. The box is padded by the warp amplitude (`cutFieldPad`), so a rock whose final outline reaches into the view is never dropped.
- The code is arithmetic only (no sin/cos/atan2/pow/hypot or `**`), with `FIELD_LAYOUT_VERSION`, so every browser cuts the server's rocks. Saved rock records are keyed by that version.
- Locks: `fieldLayout.test.ts` (same seed gives same rocks; unique paths; a sub-area cut equals the same rocks in a full cut, none missing) and `tests/unit/fieldLayoutArithmeticOnly.test.ts`.

### Chunks and rock keys (A2)

The field is split into a fixed grid of square chunks (`CHUNK_SIZE` = 4096 u, about 4 km).

- **Membership.** A rock belongs to the chunk holding its **pre-warp centre**, half-open, so every rock has exactly one chunk.
- **The key.** A rock's key is `(chunk x, chunk y, index)`, packed into one u32 (8 + 8 + 16 bits; `rockKey`, `unpackRockKey`). The index is the rock's rank by cut path within its chunk, so a rock has the same name whether one chunk or the whole field is cut, in any order.
- **Cutting one chunk.** `rocksInChunk(field, cx, cy)` cuts one chunk at full detail. It is generation-time code: the client runs it off the frame and the server on wake.
- **Measured on the full field** (seed `look`): 114,628 rocks in 3,924 chunks; a median of 21 rocks per chunk, p99 90, max 99.
- **Rule.** Changing `CHUNK_SIZE` renames every rock, so it bumps `FIELD_LAYOUT_VERSION`.
- **Locks** (`fieldLayout.test.ts`):
  - every rock of a full cut lands in exactly one chunk, and chunk cuts equal the full cut;
  - keys are unique and unpack to their chunk and index;
  - cutting a neighbour first changes nothing;
  - negative chunk coordinates pack correctly.

### In the game (behind the `cutField` room option)

Only test and showcase rooms set `cutField: { seed }`; live sectors never do until the owner has seen a fly-through.

- **Shared streamer.** `CutFieldStreamer` (`core/terrain/chunkStreamer.ts`) loads the chunks near each ship and unloads them after a linger; the server and the client both run it, so both hold the same rocks (#15). Field rocks never ride the wire; the welcome carries only the seed (#16).
- **Server:** loaded rocks are Rapier FIXED bodies (`World.fixBody`, the worker's `staticBody: 'fixed'`), not locked dynamic ones; the wire and saves skip them. Cut pieces touch along their cuts, and as locked dynamic bodies the solver worked every rock–rock contact while they were awake (see Measurements). The streamer cuts at most 2 chunks per 250 ms step on the room's main thread.
- **Chunk cuts** (`rocksInChunk`) pass `own` = the chunk, so pieces that can't hold a member rock are dropped before they are cut or finished; same rocks as cutting the padded view and filtering.
- **Client:** `net/CutFieldClient.ts` loads one chunk per 125 ms (7 × 7 chunks, ~28 km). The cutting runs in the page's art bake pool (`ArtService` `pageBakeRunner().cutChunk`, `bake.worker.ts`), at most 2 cuts out at once and ahead of queued bakes; the streamer's `cutChunk` hook returns null until a cut lands, and loads it on the next step. Without workers the pool cuts inline. The worker also packs the chunk (`core/terrain/packedChunk.ts`: keys, draw outlines, ore per vertex, and each rock's `fieldRockBody` in float64) and transfers the buffers, so a landing cut costs the main thread no structured clone and no per-rock work; a rock's body is unpacked only when it gets a predWorld collider or wakes (`packedChunk.test.ts` locks it equal to the server's `fieldRockBody`, #15). On a phone the renderer shares that main thread, so a cut there dropped frames (see Measurements). The client adds predWorld bodies only near the ship, and hands chunk outlines to the renderer through `RenderMirror.pendingFieldChunks` → `render/field/CutFieldLayer.ts`: one Mesh per chunk, each rock a fan from its centre, drawn with the owner-approved procedural rock shader (`render/field/fieldRockShader.ts`, from `exp/terrain-look`: dome, fBm, craters, cracks, lit by the sector sun, detail cut at ~2 px from the camera zoom). No triangulation; the mesh is built once per chunk.
- **Waking (C2/C3).** Field rocks are mountains (owner 2026-11-16: a fighter must not move one an inch; a battleship must not move one its own size, only nudge much smaller ones so a snagged capital gets free). Mass is `fieldRockMass(radius)` = `FIELD_ROCK_BATTLESHIP_MASSES` (100) × a battleship's mass × (radius / battleship radius)², so a battleship-sized rock weighs 100 battleships and a rock a tenth of a battleship's radius weighs one. Measured (live kinds): fighter 8, destroyer 400, battleship 7 871; rocks of seed `x` (5 785 in a 40 km box) run r 60 / 167 (p10) / 326 (median) / 10 539 (max), weighing 343 / 2 650 / 10 100 / 1.06 × 10⁷ (before: 3.4 / 26.5 / 101 / 105 702, so a fighter outweighed the smallest rocks), and a battleship-radius rock 787 141. **A rock wakes only to a body at least as heavy as itself** (`fieldRockWakes(rockMass, other, otherMass, closingSpeed)`, one shared predicate): a fighter wakes nothing (it would need a rock under r 9, the smallest cut piece is r ≈ 60), so it bounces off a still-FIXED rock exactly as off a wall, on the server and in the client's predWorld alike (the client only ever wakes a rock that arrives awake on the swarm wire); a battleship wakes rocks up to r ≈ 288 (its own mass, about the median rock), a destroyer only pieces under r ≈ 65. The contact's mass comes from the live kind: a field rock's `fieldRockMass`, an AI hull's `shipBodyMass` by its registry `shipKind`, a pilot ship's by `CutFieldRoomHooks.shipMass` (contacts name a pilot hull by playerId); anything else (asteroid, scrap, wreck, structure) has no mass here and never wakes a rock. Measured (`cutFieldRockMass.test.ts`): a battleship creeping in at 30 u/s and pressing under full thrust shoves an r 191 rock (0.44 battleships) about 165 u in 10 s while advancing about 234 u, so it pushes free; against an r 4 616 rock it stops and the rock never wakes. A woken rock is turned dynamic in the contact batch: `CutFieldSubsystem.onContact` posts `UNFIX_BODY` (`World.unfixBody`: Fixed → Dynamic, awake), at most `FIELD_WAKES_PER_TICK` (4) a tick. Anything else that must wake one (a mining laser about to cut it) calls `CutFieldSubsystem.wakeRock(id, by, tick)`: same cap, false when it is hit (retry next tick). A fixed rock never wakes a fixed neighbour (the cut pieces touch); an AWAKE rock driven into a fixed one at a closing speed of at least `FIELD_CHAIN_WAKE_SPEED` (20 u/s) wakes it (chain wake, `fieldRockWakes`) when it is at least as heavy as the fixed one (the same mass rule), so a shove spreads into lighter rocks but never into a heavier one; pressed slowly (under 20 u/s) a woken rock can pin against a still-fixed neighbour, and the capital backs off or turns. An awake rock rides the swarm wire (v7) as an asteroid with flag `SWARM_RECORD_FLAG_FIELD_ROCK` and its rock key in bytes 32–35 (chunk x, chunk y, index) instead of the resource byte and shape seed. The client asteroid leaf spawns its body from the cut outline (`CutFieldClient.wakeFieldRock`, which also drops the fixed body; a client without that chunk falls back to a generated rock), and the sprite updater draws it with the same shader as its own Mesh (`CutFieldLayer.buildRockSprite`) while its fan in the chunk mesh collapses onto its centre. A woken rock unseen on the wire for 2 s (client) / 120 frames (renderer) counts as asleep again (its server chunk unloaded and will re-cut it where it was left).
- **Deep sleep (C2).** When a woken rock's chunk unloads (no ship near), its body and slot go and `CutFieldSubsystem` keeps where it was left (`movedPose`: its live SAB centre and angle; one closer than 0.5 u and 1e-3 rad to its cut pose counts as unmoved and clears it) in the change record, saved to `terrain_records` (C4). When the chunk loads again the streamer's spawn puts it there, as a FIXED body (`AsteroidSpec.angle`; its rest frame `restX/restY`, where its ore is sampled, stays its cut centre), and marks it `fieldMoved`. Every client cuts that rock at its cut pose, so a moved rock rides the swarm wire by its key like an awake one (`fieldRockOnWire`: awake or moved; the full swarm snapshot carries it every second regardless of interest), and the client drops it from its chunk mesh and draws it at the wire pose, with no client change. A ram or laser wakes it as usual (`UNFIX_BODY`). Placement (C5) sees an unloaded moved rock where it was left. Lock: `tests/integration/sectorRoom/cutFieldDeepSleep.test.ts` (wake and move a rock, fly every ship away, come back: the server re-cuts it at the moved pose and the real `decodeSwarmPacket` sees it there), `CutFieldSubsystem.test.ts` (deep sleep), `terrainSql.test.ts` (pose round trip, the column migration). Client side (done): a client draws 7 × 7 chunks but the server loads only 5 × 5 around ships, so where no server chunk is loaded the saved pose itself travels, like consumed keys: the welcome carries every moved pose (`cutField.moved: [{k, x, y, a}]`, `FieldRockPoseSchema`, capped at `MAX_WELCOME_MOVED_ROCKS`) and each save sends one reliable `field_moved {k, x, y, a}` (`FieldMovedSchema`); a pose that goes back to the cut pose sends `field_moved {k, clear: true}`, and a break (`field_consumed`) clears it too. `CutFieldClient` keeps the poses, sends each chunk to the draw with `[index, x, y, angle]` per moved rock (`movedInChunk`; a move re-sends its loaded chunk once), and builds the rock's predWorld body at that pose (`setShipStateAsleep` after the fixed spawn). `CutFieldLayer` moves that rock's fan in the chunk mesh to the pose (`placeMovedRock`: each vertex keeps its offset from the `fieldRockBody` centre, turned by the angle; `aLocal` stays in the rock's frame, as on its woken sprite), so no extra mesh. While the rock rides the wire the woken path collapses it in the chunk (no double draw), and it comes back at the moved pose. Locks: `cutFieldDeepSleep.test.ts` (one `field_moved` with the saved pose, the joiner's welcome), `CutFieldClient.test.ts` / `CutFieldLayer.test.ts` (moved rocks), `tests/unit/cutFieldMovedPoseContract.test.ts` (#15: the server's saved pose, through the welcome, client and layer, maps back onto the cut outline with the server's `toRestFrame`). ### Ore (F1)

Ore is a map over space, not a label on a rock (owner): the piece a mining laser cuts off yields what lay where that piece sat. `core/terrain/fieldOre.ts` gives the ice and crystal concentration (0..1 each, 0 = plain rock) at any rest-frame point.

- **Loot table** (`ORE_TABLE`):
  - The field is cut into `ORE_CELL` cells (8192 u), and each cell rolls its own hotspots from `(seed, cell)`.
  - Small ice and crystal **pockets** (a few hundred u to about 1400 u) can turn up in any cell.
  - Huge, rich **lodes** (2400–6000 u, peak 0.8–1) roll at full chance inside `ORE_CORE_SHARE` (0.35) of the field radius. The chance falls to none at `ORE_RIM_SHARE` (0.75).
  - Measured on seed `look`: within 0.2 R, about 25 % of points hold ice and 12 % crystal. Past 0.7 R, 2 % hold ice and under 1 % crystal.
- **Falloff:** `hotspotConc` is a smoothstep from the peak at the centre to 0 at the edge. The edge is roughened by `vnoise` (±`ORE_EDGE_DEPTH`). Where hotspots of one resource overlap, the strongest wins. No hotspot reaches past the 3×3 cells `oreAt` reads (tested).
- **Rest frame:** ore is fixed to the rock, so a shoved or split rock carries it. Callers map a world point back through the rock's pose. Cut-field rocks spawn at angle 0.
- **Deterministic and cheap:**
  - Arithmetic only, seeded from `ore:<seed>`, a separate stream from the cuts.
  - `ORE_VERSION` bumps on any change.
  - `oreAt` allocates nothing, because the mining laser samples it on every hit (#14).
  - Nothing rides the wire: a joiner rebuilds the map from the welcome seed (#16).
- **Yield:** `oreRow(conc)` (`shared-types/asteroidResources.ts`) turns concentrations into a yield row: ice and crystal take their share of every 10 units mined, minerals the rest. Every extractor splits mined units by ONE function, `yieldInto(row, drawn, out)` (the grid Miner, robot harvesters via `OreDraw.mine`, the mining laser); a scattered rock's row is `ASTEROID_YIELD[kind]`. The Miner reads a rock's row through the `asteroidYieldOf` hook.
- **By hit point (F1b), retired:** a mining-laser hit used to pay the ore at the point it landed. Since all mined ore arrives as ore pods (owner 2026-11-17) the laser pays nothing per hit; a harvester or Miner draws the ore at the rock's centre (`CutFieldSubsystem.oreAtCentre` → `oreRow`). `oreAtRest` / `toRestFrame` stay for the pieces' ore once field rocks are cut (terrain C3). Lock: `tests/integration/sectorRoom/cutFieldOreYield.test.ts`.
- **Change record (C4, saved across restarts):** `CutFieldSubsystem` keeps, by rock key, only rocks somebody changed. `minedPool` holds what is left in a mined rock when its chunk unloads, and the re-cut spawns it with that pool, so flying away and back never refills a rock. `markConsumed(key)` / `isConsumed(key)` mark a rock that was broken up (salvage rock cut; its pieces are live wrecks), and the streamer's spawn refuses it from then on. `movedPose` holds where a woken rock was left when its chunk unloaded (deep sleep, C2, above).
  - **The save:** each change is one `TERRAIN_RECORD_PUT` on the CRITICAL lane, an absolute upsert of the rock's whole record into the `terrain_records` table keyed on (field key, rock key), where the field key is `<sectorKey>|<seed>` so a sector re-seeded with another field never reads the old field's records with the `FIELD_LAYOUT_VERSION` it was cut under: the pool left (NULL for none), the consumed flag and the moved pose (`pose_x`, `pose_y`, `pose_angle`; NULL for the cut pose; added to old tables by `ALTER`s in the `PLAYER_SHIPS_MIGRATIONS` list). It is written on `markConsumed` and when a rock's chunk unloads with a pool or a moved pose different from the one recorded, never per tick. SQL lives in `src/server/db/terrainSql.ts`, shared by the DB worker and `SyncSinkAdapter`.
  - **The load:** once, when the subsystem starts, through the main thread's read-only connection (`terrainRecordStoreFor(sectorKey)` in `PersistenceWorker.ts`, handed in as the `records` hook). Loaded consumed keys go straight into the consumed set, so the welcome's `cutField.consumed` carries them to every joiner. Rows cut under another `FIELD_LAYOUT_VERSION` name other rocks: they are ignored with one warning (count) and overwritten if the same key is written again. A failed SELECT loads nothing and warns.
  - **Who saves:** only a room with a sector key that persists its sector (`persistSector`, the same gate as the sector snapshot). Engineering rooms and test rooms with `persistSector: false` pass no `records` hook and keep the record in memory only.
  - **One store:** field rocks are never in the sector snapshot's `swarm[]` (`SectorPersistence.persist()` skips `rec.fieldRock`), so rock state lives only here.

- **Placement sees unloaded rocks (C5):** structure placement refuses a footprint on a field rock whose chunk is not loaded, as if the rock were there. `StructurePlacementSubsystem.place` passes the footprint box to the room's `getObstacles(near)` hook; with `near` the room adds `CutFieldSubsystem.dormantObstacles(box, out)`, which cuts the rocks reaching that box from the layout (`fieldRocksInBox`: `slicedField` with the box as its view, so a big rock owned by a far chunk is found, then `rocksInChunk` of each found rock's chunk for its key) and pushes each one that is neither consumed nor live in the registry (a live rock is already there, at its current pose) as its bounding circle (`fieldRockCircle`, the same x, y, radius `fieldRockBody` gives the live body) plus its real shape. **Real shape, not the circle (C5b):** a cut-field rock is big and irregular (radius up to ~8.9 km), so the circle alone refused footprints kilometres from its outline. A `GridObstacle` may carry `parts` (convex polygons relative to its centre) and an `angle`; `placementRejection` uses the circle as the broad phase and then refuses only a footprint box that overlaps a part (separating-axis box vs convex polygon; touching is legal, no extra margin, as for the circle). Dormant rocks carry the parts their collider would have (`fieldRockBody(rock).parts`, at rest, angle 0; parts null ⇒ the outline's convex hull, as Rapier builds it). Live rocks (`SectorRoom.gatherStructureObstacles`) carry `rec.wreckParts` (the field rock's collider parts; every field rock has them, see C1b) else `rec.vertices` (an ordinary asteroid's convex outline) at the live SAB pose, so a woken, turned rock is tested where it is. **Line of sight too (C5c):** a connection line (auto-connect, the 1 Hz reconnect sweep, the canConnect checks) is tested against an obstacle's box as the broad phase and, when the obstacle carries `parts`, blocked only if it crosses a part (`Grid.segmentHitsObstacle`: `rayHitsConvexPolygon` per part, turned by `angle`; allocation-free). - **Consumed keys on the wire (C3):** the consumed set only grows, and breaks are rare, so it is not a snapshot delta. Both travel in order on the same socket, so welcome + messages = the set (#16). Moved poses travel the same way (`cutField.moved` + `field_moved`, deep sleep above). A reconnect's welcome replaces it, even with the same seed. The client (`CutFieldClient`) gives a consumed rock no body. In the draw, it flattens the rock's fan onto its centre (`collapsePackedRock`) instead of leaving it out: `CutFieldLayer` finds a rock by its index in the chunk, so the count and order must not change. A break in a loaded chunk re-sends that chunk's outlines once. Locks: `CutFieldClient.test.ts` (consumed rocks), `packedChunk.test.ts`, `tests/integration/sectorRoom/cutFieldConsumed.test.ts`.
- **Look (F1c):** the rock surface shows the ore where the map has it. `oreForOutlines` samples `[ice, crystal]` at every fan vertex when a chunk loads (client, main thread); it rides `pendingFieldChunks[].ore` into the `aOre` attribute, and `fieldRockShader` draws it by style: `?ore=off|tint|veins|seams` (veins by default, the owner's pick; `?ore=off` shows bare rock; shot `cut-field-ore`). A woken rock keeps its own vertices' ore.
- **Placed rocks and their cut pieces** (a room's single rocks, kind 0 without a field key) draw with the same shader and fan data, all in ONE mesh (`render/field/rockBatch.ts`, `CutFieldLayer.rockBatch`, in the ship layer under the hulls), so their draw calls do not grow with their count (a custom-shader Mesh never joins Pixi's batch: one Mesh per rock was one draw each). `CutFieldLayer.buildRockMesh` hands out a `RockBatchMember` per rock or piece, built once: an empty Container standing where the mesh used to, which the swarm updater moves, turns, tints and hides as before. Before each render (`onRender`) the batch writes a member's vertices only when its pose, colour or visibility up to the batch's parent changed: positions into the batch frame, its axes `aXf` (the sun stays in the rock's frame) and its premultiplied colour `aTint` (`fieldRockBatchProgram`, the same shader source with those two per-vertex inputs); a hidden or detached member collapses to a point. The arrays are rebuilt only when a member comes or goes. `render/field/placedRockMesh.ts`: a placed rock is a fan of its `generateAsteroidVertices` outline in its body frame, surface-seeded from its shape seed; its resource is a uniform ore concentration (`PLACED_ROCK_ORE`, 0.7), so the same `?ore=` look applies. A cut piece keeps every vertex in its ROOT rock's rest frame (`aLocal` from the rock's fan centre, the rock's `aRock`), and its convex parts are clipped to the rock's fan wedges so the dome parameter `aUV.x` is exactly the uncut fan's (it is affine only inside one wedge): the surface does not swim when a rock splits. `aUV.y` (far-zoom thinning) stays 0. The classic tint never touches a field rock mesh (`isFieldRockMesh`). Without the field layer (headless tests) both fall back to the plain asteroid polygon. Locks: `placedRockMesh.test.ts`, `rockBatch.test.ts`, `swarmSpriteUpdater.asteroidTint.test.ts`, `swarmSpriteUpdater.rockWreck.test.ts`, `rockPieceArt.test.ts`.

### Far zoom (B4)

Zoomed far out, real rocks would be a few pixels each, and a whole field is about 1.5 million of them. So the field is drawn from a coarse cut instead.

- **Coarse cut (core, done):** `slicedField` with `minSize` stops cutting below that size. A piece stopped that way is a far-zoom piece, not a rock. It is kept whole: no rim drop and no street inset, so it covers the rocks it would have been cut into. It is roughened one level, not four (`roughen(…, levels)`), and bent by the same warp. Lane strips and the core hole are cut away as before. A piece that stops as a real rock (rock-sized or a rare big rock) is finished as a rock. Far-zoom pieces are never saved or keyed, so `FIELD_LAYOUT_VERSION` is unchanged and full-detail rocks are byte-identical (hash lock in `fieldCoarse.test.ts`).
- **Tiles:** `coarseFieldTiles(opts)` cuts the whole field at `FIELD_COARSE_MIN_SIZE` (2048 u). It groups the pieces into tiles of `COARSE_TILE_CHUNKS`² (8 × 8) chunks by pre-warp centre, the same rule `rocksInChunk` uses for chunks. `packCoarseTile` (`packedChunk.ts`) packs a tile's outlines and ore with the chunk packing (`packOutlines` + `oreForOutlines`). There are no bodies, because a far-zoom piece never collides.
- **Measured** (seed `far-zoom`, full 150 km field; coverage = drawn centres of the full-detail rocks in 6 sample chunks that lie inside a coarse piece; ms printed by the test on this box, cold, never asserted):

| | coverage | pieces | points | ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old `minSize` path (rim drop, inset, 4 rough levels) | 67.1 % (110/164) | 13,723 | 877,904 | 162 |
| far-zoom pieces (kept whole, 1 rough level) | 95.1 % (156/164) | 22,166 | 180,852 | 63–72 |

  Both lane centre lines are clear of every coarse piece. Budget: ≤ 200k points for the whole field (`fieldCoarse.test.ts`). An earlier warm measurement on other seeds gave 96–99 % coverage and 26–36 ms.
- **Bake job (client):** the art bake pool cuts the coarse field as one more job on the chunk-cut queue (`ChunkCutJobs.addCoarse`, `BakeRunner.cutCoarseField`; the worker answers `{ coarse }` with `{ tiles }`, buffers transferred). `InlineBakeRunner` cuts it on the main thread when there is no Worker. `packCoarseField` (`chunkCutJobs.ts`) is the one function both run.
- **When:** `CutFieldClient` asks once per seed, on the first step after a full-detail chunk has landed, so the ship's own rocks come first. It waits while the pool is still loading. A failed pool job is cut inline once (same tiles, #15). `stop` (sector change, disconnect) drops every tile drawn and bumps the generation, so a late answer is ignored. Tiles reach the renderer through `RenderMirror.pendingFieldChunks` with `level: 'coarse'` (cx, cy are tile coords), not a new queue.
- **Layer:** `CutFieldLayer` keeps the tiles in `coarseLayer`, under `chunkLayer`, built with `buildFieldChunkMesh` and the same shader instance as the chunks (one program). Tiles do not show consumed or moved rocks: they are approximate, and only drawn where a rock is a few pixels.
- **The switch:** `farFieldMix(scale)` (`FarZoomIcons.ts`) is 1 (tiles only) at and below `FAR_FIELD_SWITCH_SCALE` = `FAR_ICON.fadeStartPx` / 650 u (the median rock) ≈ 0.0215, 0 (chunks only) at and above that × `FAR_ICON.hysteresis` (≈ 0.0269), linear between. The two layers crossfade by it: the tiles (underneath) have alpha min(1, 2·mix), the chunks min(1, 2·(1 − mix)), so one level is always opaque. A plain mix/1 − mix fade left both half see-through at mid-band, which read as a washed-out double exposure (owner stills, 0.024). The layer at 0 is `visible = false`, so it costs no draw. It is continuous, so it cannot flicker at an edge. With no tiles yet the chunks stay full. The fighter floor (0.15) draws chunks only; the capital floors (0.0112, 0.00484) draw tiles only. Each frame, tiles outside the camera's visible bounds are hidden (`setView`'s `view`; the renderer has no Pixi culler). Nothing here allocates per frame (#14).
- **Thinning at the edge:** the real field thins toward its rim (`fieldRimKeep(d)` of the rocks kept, and wider streets), but the coarse pieces are kept whole, so without help the far level is solid out to its outline. `coarseFieldThin(d)` = 1 − `fieldRimKeep(d)` × (1 − d²) says how see-through to draw it (d = distance from the field centre / radius). The (1 − d²) street term is a fit: real rock area over coarse area, per distance band, relative to the core, tracks it within 0.67–1.56× on seeds `far-zoom` and `look` (a solid coarse level is 3–40× off from d 0.7 out; `fieldCoarse.test.ts`). `CutFieldLayer.setCoarseTile` writes 1 + thin per vertex into `aUV.y` (rock meshes keep 0 to 1, so `aUV.y` ≥ 1 also marks a far-zoom vertex), and the field shader drops the surface in rock-sized patches (value noise at 1,200 u and 520 u, remapped about uniform with smoothstep(0.15, 0.85), so the share drawn is about 1 − thin); patches under ~2 px fall back to plain alpha. The lumps and creases of the coarse pieces are unchanged.
- **Rock-sized pieces at the switch:** a coarse piece averages ~1,630 u, 2.5× the median rock (~650 u), with no streets, so the crossfade swapped ~20 px rocks with dark streets for slabs (owner, phone, command mode). Cutting deeper costs too much (`minSize` 1,448 → 387k points, 1,200 → 679k, 1,024 → 1.22M, all over the 200k budget), so the shader finishes the cut instead. `setCoarseTile` puts the piece's centre in `aRock.yz` (Pixi world = `aLocal` + `aRock.yz`; `aRock.x` stays 0). On a far-zoom pixel the shader repeats the field's cut down to rock size: up to 6 straight seeded cuts, each about square to the one before, the pixel's side choosing the next region, stopping below 0.7 × `FIELD_FAR_ROCK_CELL` (480 u, the field area per rock near the core: 455 u on seed `look`, 567 u on `far-zoom`; `fieldCoarse.test.ts`). Each sub-piece is inset by half the street (`fieldStreet(d, F)`, the one `slicedField` uses, so it widens toward the rim) and shaded as a faceted roof sloping from its nearest edge. It is full at ≥ 8 px a rock and fades to the plain piece by 4 px (scale ≈ 0.0083), so the battleship floor is unchanged. Cost: no CPU, memory or point change (181k points); on the GPU, ≤ 6 cuts (a hash each) per far-zoom pixel while it shows, not measured on a phone yet. Geometry was already right: the coarse level over-fills open space by 0.5 % (core), 1.4 % and 1.9 % (lanes) at the switch scale (offline raster, 46.5 u a pixel, seed `look`), with nothing past 20 px from the real field; the core edge has 94 cells 10–20 px out.
- **What the far level shows (stills checked against a raster of the cut, seed `look`, battleship floor):** the ~10–15 smooth light slabs are the field's rare big rocks (`bigChance`, 1–2 battleship lengths). They are real rocks, cut and drawn exactly as in the full-detail chunks, and drawn once (the chunk layer is hidden there; nothing else draws rocks at that zoom). The 30–60 px black polygons are true voids: big-rock pieces dropped by the rim thinning (each void's area is in the big-rock size range, and a full-detail cut of each holds no rock). The thin dark creases are the slivers between neighbouring coarse pieces, each roughened on its own.
- **Memory (measured, seeds `far-zoom` and `test-seed`):** 87–88 tiles, ~205k fan vertices (≤ 3.7k a tile), ~12 MB of mesh arrays kept on the CPU and the same again in GPU buffers. The packed tiles (~3.2 MB) cross from the worker once per sector. Cut 49–75 ms in the worker (this box, cold).
- **Draw calls (count gate, estimated):** one per visible tile. A tile is 8 × 4,096 u ≈ 32.8 km on a side, so a battleship's phone floor (~85 × 184 km) meets at most 4 × 7 = 28 tiles, fewer at the field's edge and over the core hole. Below the switch the chunk meshes cost none. Inside the band both levels draw.
- **Measured (phone-proxy, 2026-11-17):** scene `cut-field-far` A/B GREEN: frame p95 16.7 → 16.7 ms, render p95 0.9 → 0.9 ms, display objects 1,381 → 1,470. The owner approved the look from stills (rock-size shards, thinned rim). The camera streaming ring is unchanged: it still cuts up to 13 × 13 chunks around the camera when zoomed far out, though they are hidden there.

## The core idea

A rock that nobody has touched is not data. Server and client recompute it from its field's seed with shared, arithmetic-only code. Only changes become records: a moved, mined or destroyed rock, or a battle wreck piece.

Every rock and wreck piece sits at one of three sleep levels in the same swarm registry:

- **Awake:** body plus slot.
- **Asleep:** body plus slot, `FLAG_SLEEPING`.
- **Deep asleep:** no body, no slot, just its saved record. A rock nobody has touched has no record at all.

For cut-field rocks today: a loaded, untouched rock is a fixed body off the wire; a woken one is awake (dynamic, on the wire); a moved rock whose chunk unloaded is deep asleep (its pose in the change record), and comes back as a fixed body on the wire where it was left (C2).

## Stage 1: deep sleep

### 1a: a rock's shape comes from its stable id

A rock must keep its outline when it leaves the world and comes back (deep sleep), so its shape cannot depend on its wire id, which is reused.

- **Seed:** each rock's shape seed is `asteroidShapeSeed(rec.id)`, a u16 hash of its stable registry id. It is computed once at spawn (`SwarmSpawner`) and kept on the record as `rec.shapeSeed`.
- **Wire:** the seed rides the swarm wire in the u16 at +34 (`SWARM_REC_SHAPE_SEED_OFF`), the same bytes a wreck piece uses for its `wreckId` and that a rock left empty. Because the meaning of those bytes changed for rocks, `SWARM_WIRE_VERSION` went 5 → 6: an old client would have drawn the wrong outlines.
- **Client:** the decoder keeps the seed on the mirror entry, and the prediction collider, the sprite and the baked art all build from it. `FORGE_VERSION` bumped, so rock art re-bakes.
- **Determinism:** `generateAsteroidVertices` uses arithmetic only.
  - Directions come from `unitDirFromSteps` (`core/geometry/unitDir.ts`). This is the Taylor-series helper the capital fracture already used, moved there so there is one copy.
  - `Math.sin`/`Math.cos` are not bit-exact across JS engines. Field rocks will be derived on the client, so every browser must produce the server's vertices.
- **Decoder fix:** an asteroid record now clears a stale `asteroidResource` when a plain rock reuses a wire id.
- **Locks:**
  - `SwarmSpawner.test.ts`: an outline survives a different wire id.
  - `tests/unit/swarmWireRoundTrip.test.ts`: the seed round-trips, and a reused id keeps no old seed or resource.
  - `unitDir.test.ts`: within 1e-12 of cos/sin at every step.
