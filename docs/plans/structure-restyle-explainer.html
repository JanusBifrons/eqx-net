Structure restyle & articulation

Bases are being rebuilt from scratch, starting with a small refining outpost. They should be big enough to sit beside capital ships, readable at a glance as one owner's, and full of moving parts. This page explains the plan section by section. Every drawing here is a wireframe of intent, not final art.

Branch feat/structure-restyle · local only · no PR until you sign off

Look → motion → gameplay

Each stage ends with something you can see and approve before the next one starts. Gameplay changes come last, once the look and the animation are right.

§1 · Why

Bases are toys next to the ships

Today's Capital structure is 560 units across, a tenth of a battleship. The new ladder puts each tier where it belongs. A capital structure dwarfs a battleship, and a small one is still a few fighters across.

Ships in ink, new structure tiers in amber (ranges), today's Capital in red outline.
§2 · First deliverable

The small refining outpost

One beacon at the root. Hexagonal nodes branch off it, and buildings hang off the nodes as leaves. Ore comes in through the intake dock and moves through the network to the refinery. Refined goods go back out through the outlet dock. Lines run underneath, and ships fly over them.

The red ring and panels mark livery and sigil areas, which every building gets. The dashed arc is each dock's crane at rest.
§2 · Shapes

No squares. Hex nodes you glance off

Today every structure's collider is a plain box. A ship that clips a flat corner-on face stops dead. With hexagonal nodes and faceted, irregular buildings, most hits become glancing blows, and you slide along instead of stopping. The same outline drives both the art and the collider, so what you see is what you hit.

§4 · Stage 1 choice

Three style directions to sketch

Each direction will be built for all six outpost pieces and shown as in-game stills beside a fighter, a hauler and a destroyer. These thumbnails show only the silhouette idea. You pick one, or mix them.

A · Faceted industrial

Chamfered hex plates, heavy plating, painted livery bands, hazard paint.

B · Clustered modules

Irregular hex and round pods joined by trusses. Reads as a station grown over time.

C · Shipyard brutalist

Big asymmetric slabs, angled cuts, one dominant livery panel. Closest to the capital-ship look.

Line looks (still under ships, fly over)

The moving dashes show flow: amber for ore and power, cyan for refined goods. Final colours follow the material.
§3 · The dock

Three layers, one building

The game has a single physics plane, so "height" is just draw order plus who collides with what. The striped lip is painted under ships, and you fly over it. The building is solid. The crane is drawn above ships and passes over them without touching. The new medium dock is the existing Freight Dock restyled and extended, so it keeps its trading features.

Side view for explanation only. In game it's top-down; the crane is drawn on top of the ship.

Crane sequence (intake)

Intake dock (drop-off)

  • Amber chevrons point into the base.
  • The lit sign shows an arrow into a box, plus the cargo icon.
  • It takes only what the dock's filter allows (set per dock).
  • Stop for about 1 s and the arm comes out by itself. Fly off and it aborts.

Outlet dock (pick-up)

  • Cyan chevrons point out of the base.
  • The lit sign shows an arrow out of a box, plus the cargo icon.
  • It fills your hold to the brim, or until the base runs out.
  • Same trigger: stop and wait.
§5 · Stage 2

Articulation: moving parts for anything

Weapon mounts are already turning joints that the server drives. The new system grows that idea instead of building a second one. A mount becomes one kind of part. New parts can slide or swing on a timed clip, like a short matinee. Parts can carry other parts, so a pop-up turret is a slide part with the existing mount riding on it. An arm can also be told to reach a point in the world.

  • Two kinds of part. Cosmetic parts are drawn only. Physical parts are real moving bodies that push things predictably, like a door.
  • Reach. The arm is solved to touch a target point: the parked ship's hold.
  • Not structure-only. Barge side doors and pop-out ship weapons will use the same parts later. They are out of scope for now.
  • Gates and airlocks keep their instant on/off walls for now. Moving them onto this system is a separate job later.

What the sandbox clip will show

  • A door opens, a turret slides out, then both go back in.
  • A hauler stops in the dock, and the arm reaches, clamps, transfers and retracts.
  • The ship leaves mid-reach, and the arm aborts cleanly.
  • Edge cases: the ship is off-centre, drifting, or there are two ships in the bay.

  • Uploaded PNG sigils: storage, size limits and moderation.
